# Derbis 1.5.0

**109 commits since v1.1.0.** Liquid Glass became a real material instead of a tinted film,
two player decks arrived and two were retired, the notch became a surface that moves, listening
gained lossless output and Dolby Atmos, Spotify Canvas learned to sign in from inside Derbis,
Discord Connect learned to actually open Discord, and the year reel stopped refusing the year
you are living in.

| Version code | `3000213` |

| Checksum | `Derbis.apk.sha256`, uploaded next to the APK |
| Upgrades | Same package and release keystore as v1.1.0, and a higher version code, so it installs straight over it |

Verify the download before installing:

```bash
sha256sum -c Derbis.apk.sha256
```

This build is stable (`1.5.0`, no prebuild name), so its in-app updater only ever sees `v*`
releases — it will not offer a test prebuild again.

## What's New

### Liquid Glass is now actual glass

- The page is recorded into a backdrop layer, and every bar, dock, mini player, sheet and player
  control samples it through the Kyant0/backdrop engine: vibrancy, 8 dp blur, rim highlight, soft
  shadow and — on Android 13 and newer — lens refraction with chromatic aberration, all under one
  neutral 40% surface film. Artwork and text warp under the surface instead of sitting behind tint.
  The old blurred `RenderEffect` frost is gone; a surface is either that glass or a flat tint with an
  edge-light rim, which is also what Android 11 and older get.
- The skin's palette panels are a glass film too, so every card, row, sheet and chip is translucent.
  Content drawn *inside* the page keeps the lightweight material on purpose: a card cannot sample the
  page it is being drawn into, so it gets the same tint and the same light-catching rim.
- The whole bottom chrome is one glass group — the current tab's orb, the player pill and a search
  orb. Scrolling the dock fuses them; the orbs grow out of the pill's edges and the wide dock shrinks
  away, and the leading orb expands it again.
- Cost scales with the device. Low-RAM and battery-saver phones keep a coarser frost with no lens,
  and content rows and page surfaces sample a cheaper bucket than chrome.
- The full-page film no longer re-blurs the backdrop every frame, which removes the single most
  expensive glass surface in the app.

### Two new player decks, and two retirements

- **Derbis One** is the paper deck: a scalloped mat cut from the artwork with one 240-sample closed
  path (no image asset), a thin orbit around it that *is* the progress ring, and a dot on that orbit
  as the playhead. Dragging the artwork scrubs; the clock above it reads in tabular mono. Title in
  caps over an `ALBUM by ARTIST` kicker, five controls on one line, and a Lyrics strip with the real
  dock actions on its trailing edge.
- **Frost** is the glass deck for the Liquid Glass skin: the artwork's own accent is the light behind
  a near-black ground, the sleeve floats on that glow, and title, seek and transport sit on one pane
  of glass with a single specular hairline along its top edge. Its seek fill runs from the artwork
  accent to white, and the thumb carries its own rim.
- Both honour the animations switch and fall back to the default player for video, live radio,
  side-by-side and empty states. Both are selectable from **Settings → Interface → Player deck** and
  from the deck sheet.
- The deck chooser is now six cover-led players: **Derbis Flow, Sleeve, Apple Music 13, Late Night,
  Frost, Derbis One**. Liner Notes and Folio are deleted rather than hidden — a saved choice of
  either lands on **Sleeve** instead of the gallery fallback.

### The Derbis Notch

- The island sits at the top of the app as a black capsule that opens into a card with the artwork,
  the track and the synced lyrics, in a Default or Spicy lyric style.
- It moves. Capsule and card are one surface that morphs — width, height and corner radius settle on
  the same spring — while the blurred cover blooms in from behind the cutout and the two layouts
  cross-fade instead of swapping. It drops out of the cutout when a track starts and back into it
  when the player opens. A horizontal swipe on the collapsed capsule skips forward or back with the
  same carry-and-settle motion the mini player uses, and every part snaps instead of gliding when
  animations are off.
- It is off until you ask for it, has its own section in Settings (plain switch plus the Default /
  Spicy choice), can be placed rather than only configured, hides while a sheet is open, clamps its
  expanded card to the screen width, collapses after opening the player, and stops redrawing its
  waveform while playback is paused. Its playback indicator is one straight hairline along the
  bottom edge, square ended, gliding between ticks instead of stepping.

### Audio

- **Lossless output**, ported from BitChord. Derbis knows whether the connected route can carry a
  source's samples untouched — wired, USB, HDMI and dock always count; Bluetooth only on LDAC,
  LHDC, aptX Lossless or SSC UHQ — and runs a 24-bit float sink there instead of truncating
  everything to 16-bit. It stays 16-bit on the phone speaker, whenever skip-silence is on (that
  processor only takes PCM16) and on Samsung, whose vendor FLAC decoder is unsafe with float.
- **Dolby Atmos**, also from BitChord. Derbis asks the renderer itself — Media3's decoder list first,
  the platform codec list as a backstop — whether this phone has an E-AC-3 decoder, and keeps the
  answer for the session. An **Audio → Dolby Atmos** switch decides whether a source may hand over
  the E-AC-3 JOC mix: on by default where the decoder exists, greyed with the reason where it does
  not. The resolver skips Atmos candidates that would not be wanted, and the player badge names
  **DOLBY ATMOS** instead of folding a 768 kbps JOC stream into Hi-Fi.
- **Streaming quality has four steps**: Low, Medium, High, Lossless. Lossless outranks every lossy
  step on the source ladder and keeps the 320 kbps HQ upgrade on, so a FLAC or ALAC source is chosen
  whenever the provider or the local library has one. Auto is gone; a saved Auto reads as High.
- **The badge reports what you are hearing**, not the container carrying it: a lossless source reads
  **LOSSLESS**, the same source at 24-bit or 88.2 kHz and up reads **HI-FI**, and a 320 kbps HQ
  upgrade reads **HI-FI** with its bitrate. AAC and Opus keep their names below that, and the
  technical audio sheet still names the real codec, the file's bit depth and the route's verdict.
- **Karaoke**: **Settings → Audio → Karaoke** cancels the centre of the stereo mix so the lead vocal
  drops out. It is a real-time `AudioProcessor` in the playback chain, not a render job, so it toggles
  instantly on any song, and the centre's low band is kept intact so the kick and bass survive. A mono
  master has no centre and passes through untouched.

### Spotify

- **Canvas unlocks from a real Spotify web session.** Connect now signs in inside Derbis: a
  full-screen sheet opens on Spotify's own login page, the captured cookie sits in the bottom-left
  corner (tap to copy), Cancel and Use this account sit on the bottom right, and a Developer button in
  the top right slides an `sp_dc` paste field up from the bottom. A finished sign-in closes the sheet
  back to the settings card.
- The bearer is minted from that session the way the web player mints its own — an offscreen WebView
  on `open.spotify.com` that harvests the token its own JavaScript produces — and Derbis then fetches
  the track's Canvas from Spotify's `canvaz-cache` endpoint with a mobile client's user agent. No
  OAuth application of ours is involved, so no client id or redirect has to be registered anywhere.
- **Spotify is one card in Settings and one card in the session sheet.** The account row and the
  Canvas row were the same question asked twice; the card now carries the account state on top, what
  it unlocks underneath, and the single Connect / Disconnect action on the right.

### Discord

- **Sign-in is one tap.** The login sheet, its Normal/Token tabs and the embedded browser are gone:
  Connect opens Discord's own authorization page in the Discord app, falling back to the browser, and
  the account comes back through `discord-<application id>:/authorize/callback` carrying a nonce that
  is checked before the code is exchanged. No password or token is ever typed into Derbis.
- **Rich Presence reads "Listening in Derbis"**: the activity keeps the clickable Play/Artist buttons,
  the live progress bar and the opt-in lyric line, and falls back to the Derbis logo when a track has
  no artwork.

### Listening Wrapped

- Three cards pulled from SimpMusic's reel: **the albums you lived in** (the three albums that took
  the most time, with play counts), **your biggest day** (the single local day with the most
  listening, compared against a typical active day and naming the track that carried it) and **your
  listening shape** (one archetype — the Regular, the Omnivore, the Devotee or the Deep Diver — chosen
  from four measured axes: consistency, variety, devotion and replay).
- **The receipt card can be shared from any page**, not just the finale: the story header has a share
  button that renders the same card as a 1080 px PNG.
- **The running year is no longer a dead end.** The reel used to require thirty listening days before
  it would show a card, which is right for a finished year and wrong for the one you are in. The
  running year now plays as a progress report: the header chip reads `REPLAY '2026 · SO FAR`, the intro
  names the date the numbers stop, and every card is exactly the data that exists. The thirty-day
  floor still applies to a finished year, and the header gains a year picker that only offers the
  running year plus the years that can actually be replayed, so no chip opens a dead end.
- Copy sizes scale with page height, pages scroll instead of clipping on short screens, and the
  progress bar reports page position to accessibility services.

### Mix Studio

- Beat Sync is readable: each handoff shows the detected OUT and IN BPM, the exact tempo correction on
  a ±10% rail, and whether that pair can actually lock.
- Half- and double-time pairs match, so 62 BPM locks to 128 BPM instead of being refused; long
  overlaps start on a bar line while short ones snap to the nearest beat; and the handoff watcher
  checks the outgoing position every 20 ms instead of 50 ms.

### Share card

- The card is coloured by the artwork instead of by a theme. All three themes — Aura Glow, OLED Pure
  and Vinyl Studio — draw the same background from the cover's palette: a base gradient mixed toward
  the dominant colour, three blooms, and a legibility floor that keeps white title text readable on
  bright covers. OLED keeps the palette quietest and Vinyl the deepest.
- The quality badge is one long smooth pill with a two-stop fill at full radius instead of a bordered
  chip, its label comes from the snapshot's quality tier instead of being written into the layout, and
  its seven-bar mark is measured against the pill instead of positioned by hand.
- The sheet shows the live card in a fitted stage with a light sweep while it renders, format tabs
  with their export ratio, theme tiles previewing each look, and one consistent button style. Labels
  follow the app language, and noisy toasts are replaced by in-place button states.

### Settings and updates

- **Animated Artwork has exactly one control.** The switch in **Settings → Interface** is the only
  thing that turns it on, and it now updates the saved interface snapshot and the canvas network
  policy too, so the switch and the engine cannot disagree. The Spotify panel keeps the session state
  and the `sp_dc` door but no longer carries a second switch.
- **The update detector knows which release line the installed build is on.** A release is parsed into
  a line, a version and the commit it was cut from: a stable install only sees `v` releases, a test
  install prefers prebuilds and falls back to stable, and ordering uses the shared version comparator,
  so `1.6.0-prebuild.2` outranks `1.6.0-prebuild.1` and `1.6.0` outranks both. Checks run at most once
  every six hours, a successful check is remembered, a failed one is not, and a dismissal is
  remembered by release tag, not by version name.
- The "Like Derbis?" star prompt is monochrome: one hairline ring and a flat star instead of a gold
  sticker, floating mini stars and a gold glow, with the call to action white on black in dark theme.

## What's Fixed

- **Discord Connect now actually opens the Discord app.** The hand-off checked `resolveActivity()`
  before launching, and since Android 11 that answers null for every package the app cannot see — so
  every Discord candidate was skipped, and the panel fell through to a browser tab and then sat on a
  disabled "Connecting Discord" row forever. It now launches first and catches
  `ActivityNotFoundException`, trying the deep link in the named Discord builds, then any other
  handler of `discord://`, then the page in a browser; a `<queries>` block lets the panel say whether
  Discord is installed at all; and the waiting card is tappable, carries **Open Discord again** and
  **Cancel**, and expires after five minutes instead of being a state no control could leave.
- **Discord sign-in uses the redirect Discord actually accepts for mobile.**
  `derbis-discord://callback` was an arbitrary custom scheme the authorize page rejects before the
  consent screen, so even a verified Discord application could not link. It now uses
  `discord-<application id>:/authorize/callback` end to end — the URL, the manifest filter that
  receives it, and the code-for-token exchange that repeats it byte for byte — and a build without the
  Discord Social SDK signs in and says so instead of showing an account with no activity.
- **Liquid Glass draws glass instead of a flat film.** The page layer used to wrap the navigation bar,
  the mini player and the dock as well, so each of them was sampling the layer it was being painted
  into: the draw failed, the safety guard went off, and the whole app sat on the flat tinted film.
  Only the page is recorded now, and the chrome samples a layer already finished for the frame. Low
  RAM and battery saver get the cheap tier rather than switching the material off.
- **The glass GPU path no longer takes the app down**, and the guard no longer punishes a normal exit:
  it takes two consecutive foreground deaths to pause the material, the pause is fifteen minutes, a
  marker written by a different build is ignored, and a clean background session clears it.
- **Lyric karaoke follows the vocal word by word instead of lagging it.** Three timing faults stacked
  up: a line-only source spread its words across the whole line, and a line ends where the *next* one
  starts, so the tail of breath and instrumental gap pushed every word late; the sync clock corrected
  a standing lag 48 ms per player report, which never cleared it; and the word highlight read exactly
  on its timestamp while the colour tween takes 115 ms to finish, so it completed after the syllable.
  Word timings are now estimated from the delivery itself, the clock removes half the error per report
  and snaps outright past 200 ms, and the highlight reads 55 ms ahead. Provider word and syllable
  timings still win outright.
- **Apple Music 13's inline lyric row is pinned**, so it can no longer overlap the seek bar.
- **The lyric page no longer ghosts the player through it**, and the bar only slides to the screen edge
  when fullscreen is on.
- **The Replay story no longer blocks the year you are living in** (see What's New), and its receipt
  palette resolves outside the suspend call, so the card renders instead of failing quietly. The
  biggest-day tiles use a real plays label rather than a placeholder.
- **Two rounds of compile errors that the release build exposed are fixed**, so the shipped APK is
  built from the same code the prebuilds were.

## Changed

- **Beat Sync is gone from both places it existed.** The **Settings → Audio** switch that promised to
  beat-align any queue was removed — it could only align tracks that had already been played, so it
  silently did nothing on most handoffs. Mix Studio keeps its own per-transition option, where the pair
  is known and the grids are shown before you commit. The immersive Beat Sync player deck (the
  Aura/Velvet canvas style) was retired too, and an old saved choice lands on the sleeve deck instead
  of leaving the player blank; its neon beat ring, blurred-artwork wash and wavy seek bar went with it.
- **The Cast button is the glyph alone.** It sat in a glass circle — a fill plus a hairline ring — which
  put a small ring on the artwork next to the player's other round controls. Every place that shows a
  Cast action now draws it with no circle and no border, keeping the touch target and the icon.
- **The Liquid Glass player no longer draws a written-by credit line over the cover.** The credits
  eyebrow and its floating caption are suppressed on that skin only — every other skin keeps them, and
  Track credits is still one tap away in the player actions sheet.
- **Liquid Glass has no tuning panel any more.** The Theme Studio's glass section is gone — no Auto /
  Always on / Off, no blur, transparency, tint or refraction sliders — because the material is now the
  fixed Apple-matched recipe and the skin is the only switch that matters.
- **The Derbis Notch is off by default** and has its own settings section instead of being buried
  between haptics and gestures.
- **The custom SVG glyph set was reverted** to Material Rounded icons.
- The player draws one drifting artwork backdrop: the separate background picker and the raymarched
  backdrop behind it are gone.
- Test suites for decks that are no longer shipped were dropped.
