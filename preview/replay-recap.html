Replay — the listening recap screen

Pixel skin applied. The switch at the bottom right turns the skin off and redraws this whole screen on the default skin. The four periods, the highlight set and the timeline rule do not change — only colour, corners and type.

Replay is the screen you open to see a period of listening laid out as numbers: seven days, thirty days, the last year, or all of it. It has never been drawn in a preview. This is it, in Pixel, with the four periods switchable because the period changes the screen — not just the figures, but which chart is drawn and what its title says.

Every card here is a hard rectangle with a 2 dp edge, the same grammar as the rest of the skin. The one live element is the period strip: tap it and the hero, the highlights and the timeline follow.

1 — The screen, at four periods

Tap a period. The hero badge, the figure, the highlight set and the timeline title all change — because they are all computed from the same period, and a screen that only changed its numbers would be lying about the rest.

What changes with the period
PartBehaviour
Badge Uppercased period label, so the hero always names what it is counting.
Hero figure Total minutes for the period, minus anything the period did not have.
Mini stats Plays, artists, and the current streak — all re-sliced to the period.
Highlights Completion, most active day, discovery rate, daily average, and the most-replayed track.
Timeline Seven and thirty days draw daily bars with weekday labels. A year and all-time draw recent activity over the last thirty days, and the subtitle says so — a 365-bar chart is not readable at this width.
Top lists Tracks, artists and albums are ranked per period. Album rows are square; artists are round.
The one real design decision

A year cannot be drawn day-by-day in a phone-width chart — 365 bars in ~320 dp is under a pixel each. Rather than draw something unreadable, the long periods switch the chart to the last thirty days and say so in the subtitle. The rest of the screen still counts the whole period.

That is the honest version of the trade-off: the chart is the only part that narrows, and it declares it. The alternative — averaging a year into twelve bars — would have been tidier and would have hidden the detail.

2 — When there is nothing to show

A period with no listening is a normal state, not an error. It gets a card that says so, and the period strip stays usable so you can step to one that has data.

□ □ □
Nothing for this period

No plays landed in these seven days. Pick a longer period, or start listening and this page fills itself in.

  • Not a spinner, not an error. The empty card is a settled state, so the screen never looks like it is still loading.
  • The strip keeps working. You can move to 30 days or all time from here without going back.
  • Same geometry. The empty card sits where the hero sits, so the screen does not jump when data arrives.

3 — Pixel on this screen

GroundBlush paper #FFEDF1; cards are #FFF8F6.
Cards2 dp edge #A84765, 0 dp corners. No shadow, no glass.
HeroRose badge outline, plum figure, edge rule under it.
ChipsSelected = hot fill with plum ink; unselected = edge outline with muted ink.
BarsEdge at 30 % for the body of the chart, hot for the one peak.
CoversSquare for tracks and albums, round for artists — the shape carries the type, not a label.

Honest limit. This is a drawn mock at phone width, not a screenshot, and the figures are invented so each layout is exercised at a realistic length. The structure — four periods, hero plus three mini stats, five highlights, a timeline that switches between daily and recent, three ranked lists — is read from the screen's source, and so is the daily-versus-recent rule.
