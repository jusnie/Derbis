Player audio chip

Two separate things were wrong with the line under the seek bar, and only one of them is cosmetic. The badge showed the raw stream figures — AAC · 128 kbps · 44.1 kHz · 2.0 — so it said nothing a listener could act on and got longer or shorter whenever a number did. And the row it sits in gives both clocks their natural width and centres the badge in whatever is left, so every time a clock gained a digit the badge slid sideways.

The fix is a tier plus a fixed slot. The badge reads AAC · Medium, using the same Low / Medium / High bands the audio settings already offer, and all three cells of the row are given a fixed width — so the only thing that can still change is the text inside a box that cannot move.

2 — Live: the same row, locked

Identical clock and identical tier changes, but every cell has a width it cannot leave. The badge holds the same 132 px slot whether it is empty, reading AAC · Medium, or switching to AAC · High — and an empty slot renders nothing at all rather than inventing a bitrate.

Both are driven by one loop, so the drift numbers are measured from the same clock ticks. The left-hand row is what ships; the right-hand row is the proposal.

4 — The tiers

The bands are the ones the audio settings already promise, so the badge and the setting agree instead of being two vocabularies. PLAYER_HIFI_MIN_KBPS is already 256, which is why High starts there.

BandBitrateBadge readsThe setting it matches
Low below 96 kbps AAC · Low Low · 48 kbps data saver
Medium 96 – 255 kbps AAC · Medium Medium · 98 – 128 kbps balanced
High 256 kbps and up AAC · High High · 300+ kbps (320 HQ upgrade)
Lossless FLAC / ALAC / PCM FLAC · Lossless Lossless — off the bitrate ladder
Hi-Fi lossless at 24-bit / 88.2 kHz+ FLAC · Hi-Fi kept as it was — it is not a bitrate tier

A lossless stream keeps Lossless or Hi-Fi rather than a band, because its bitrate is a compression ratio and not a quality figure — that rule already existed and is preserved. The sample rate and channel count did not disappear: they are still in the technical sheet, which is the screen that exists to carry them.

5 — Why 132 dp, and why it scales

A fixed slot only works if the longest label fits inside it — otherwise the fix silently turns into an ellipsis. The longest label the banding can produce is FLAC · Lossless, so that is what the slot is cut for, not the common AAC · Low.

LabelText at 11 spPill incl. icon + paddingIn a 132 dp slot
AAC · Low59 dp88 dpfits
AAC · Medium90 dp119 dpfits
AAC · High66 dp95 dpfits
FLAC · Lossless99 dp128 dpfits, with 4 dp to spare
DOLBY ATMOS73 dp102 dpfits

The widths are multiplied by the user's fontScale at the call site. A dp slot that stays fixed while the type grows is a slot that starts clipping the moment someone turns text size up, so at 1.3× the badge slot is 172 dp and each clock is 60 dp — still inside a 360 dp phone. The numbers above are Roboto-class estimates at 0.60 em per character, which is deliberately pessimistic; Android's default face is narrower, so the real slack is larger than shown.
